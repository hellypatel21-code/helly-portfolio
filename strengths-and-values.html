Community Builders

Why CBs matter to me

Community Builders matter because a residence hall does not become a community just because people were assigned to the same hallway. Most residents will not knock on a stranger's door on their own, so a CB gives them a reason to be in the same room and something to do with their hands while they talk. I have noticed that the conversations that actually matter rarely happen when you sit people down to have them. They happen sideways, while everyone is busy with something else.

The CB I hosted this semester was a claw clip bedazzling night. Everyone picked a clip, covered it in rhinestones and glitter, and somewhere in the middle of it the room stopped being a floor meeting and turned into people talking about classes, roommates, and home. That is exactly what I hope residents take from the CBs I host: a low-pressure reason to show up, one new face they recognize in the hallway afterward, and the sense that this floor is a place where they are welcome to just exist for an hour. The craft is honestly an excuse. The point is that nobody has to walk in with a reason to be there.

Claw Clip Bedazzling Night

The setup

Plain claw clips, rhinestones, glue, and a table everyone could crowd around.

Why it worked

Low cost, no skill required, and everyone left with something they actually use.

What I would change

More time. People stayed well past when I planned to wrap up.

Next up

Something food-based, since that is the fastest way to get a floor into one room.

Personal Mission & Values

What I am actually trying to do here

I moved from Gujarat to a campus where I did not know a single person, and the thing that made it survivable was not a program or a resource guide. It was a handful of people who noticed me before I knew how to ask for anything. That is the whole reason I became an RA, and it is the standard I hold myself to: be the person who notices first.

Notice before you are asked

Most people will not tell you they are struggling. They will go quiet, stop coming out of their room, or stop answering the group chat. Paying attention is a skill, and I would rather over-check on someone than find out later I missed it.

Treat people with dignity, even in the hard moments

Enforcing a policy and respecting someone are not opposites. How a person feels walking away from a difficult conversation with me matters as much as the outcome of it.

Make room for people who are far from home

I know what it is like to be the person who does not get the reference, does not know the system, and is figuring it out in a second language of customs. I want my floor to be easy to belong to.

Build things that outlast me

Whether it is a project or a floor, I care more about whether it still works when I am not in the room. The best version of my job is residents who look out for each other without needing me to arrange it.

My mission is simple: leave people more sure that they belong here than I found them. Everything else — the programs, the paperwork, the duty nights — is in service of that.
